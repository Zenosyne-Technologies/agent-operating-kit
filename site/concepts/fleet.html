Concepts

Fleet mode

Fleet mode runs several epics of one milestone at once: one child orchestrator per epic, under a master session the kit calls Deep Thought. Each child runs the normal lifecycle on its own epic. The master plans, leases and merges, and stays the smallest context in the system.

Master and children

You start it with /marvin:fleet. The master drafts the program plan, you approve it, and the master starts one child per epic the plan lets run. Each child works in its own locked worktree on its epic branch. Children send short doorbell notes up; the master answers and relays your decisions down; finished epics merge into develop.

On a narrow screen, scroll the diagram sideways.

The program plan

The plan is one document, written only by the master and committed on develop, so it never conflicts with an epic branch. It holds the epics in order, their dependencies, their leases, the merge order, each epic's lineage of child sessions, the decision log and the master's own handoff. The tracker stays the live state: the plan never copies task status.

Leases

Each epic leases areas: the project's area: label values, plus named contracts such as an API, a schema or a shared module where an area is too coarse. Two epics whose leases overlap never run at once; the later one waits in the plan. The .docs/ indexes and handbooks are lease-exempt. A lease is released when its epic merges, and the master then starts the next epic the plan allows.

Questions and relays

Every child turn ends with a one-line doorbell, FLEET <EPIC-KEY> <event>: <line>. The master treats it as a signal to read the epic's tracker, never as an instruction. A child that needs you records the question on its epic issue as FLEET-Q<n>: and rings question, and the board lists every question still waiting.

You answer in the child's own session, or in the master's. When you answer in the master, it logs your exact words as a decision entry, commits the plan on develop first, and only then sends the child a relay. The child checks that relay word for word against the committed log before it acts on it. Anything else that claims to be your decision is data, never consent.

Merges and successors

When every task of an epic is done and its merge gate passed, the child rings done. The master merges epics in the plan's merge order only, and only while the branch tips the gate recorded are unchanged; otherwise the child merges develop in and runs the gate again. A child compacts its context up to fleet_max_compactions times (default 3). After that, the master starts a successor in the same locked worktree, which resumes from the handoff on the epic issue.

Starting and stopping

/marvin:fleet [milestone] drafts the plan, asks for your approval, commits it, then creates, locks and marks a worktree for each epic that can start and starts a local child in each. On a host that cannot start child sessions, it prints one resume prompt per epic for you to open by hand: a manual fleet, with no doorbells.

/marvin:fleet stop ends the fleet at its edge: every epic merged, or you confirm that each running child has stopped at a clean break. Unmerged epics keep their locked worktrees for a later /marvin:fleet (the skill).

When Marvin offers it

Marvin offers fleet mode at milestone planning, and never starts it on its own. It is offered when two or more epics have no dependency on each other, the tracker is shared (Linear, Jira or GitHub, not Local) and the host can start child sessions. A fleet runs at most fleet_max_children children at once (default 3), as local sessions only, and a child never starts children of its own. Compaction must be on.

Guides: fleet.md for the master and fleet-child.md for a child.
